The science behind the signal.

What NASA satellites actually measure, how we analyze their records, and where the evidence stops.

SCIENCE NOTE / 001

What is a satellite fire detection?

NASA FIRMS distributes active-fire and thermal-anomaly observations from instruments including VIIRS and MODIS. An individual detection is an observation associated with a satellite pixel, not a surveyed flame perimeter or an official fire incident report.

What does FRP mean?

Fire radiative power (FRP), when available, estimates the instantaneous radiant energy output associated with the detected heat source, reported in megawatts. FRP is not ambient air temperature, flame temperature or a direct measurement of area burned. FireTrace sums FRP only as a descriptive property of the selected records; the total should not be interpreted as total energy released over a season.

Can it detect bush burning?

Vegetation and agricultural fires can produce thermal signals detectable by VIIRS and MODIS. But a hotspot alone cannot establish whether the source is crop-residue burning, grassland burning, forest fire, an industrial heat source or something else. Attribution requires land-cover context, imagery and, ideally, local verification.

Can it show affected communities?

FireTrace currently shows nearby reference towns using a geographic distance calculation. This is useful for directing further investigation, but it is not a measure of land burned, smoke transport, household exposure or confirmed community impact. Administrative district polygons and validated population-exposure models would be required for stronger claims.

Why might detections be missing?

Clouds, satellite overpass timing, spatial resolution, fire size, canopy cover, algorithms and processing latency can all affect detection. Zero records in a chosen window does not guarantee the absence of fire.

How is the data used?

The server requests NASA FIRMS CSV data for a selected bounding rectangle, satellite dataset and one-to-five-day window. The browser parses returned coordinates, observation times, satellite metadata, confidence fields and FRP where present. Location filters use broad geographic windows rather than district boundaries. Archive CSV imports can also be analyzed locally.

Data access

NASA FIRMS requires a valid MAP_KEY for many API requests. Our server-side endpoint reads NASA_FIRMS_MAP_KEY from the hosting environment, so the key is not included in public frontend source. Uploaded CSV records are processed in the user's browser and are not uploaded to FireTrace's server.

FireTrace is an independent hackathon research project using publicly documented NASA resources. It is not operated by or endorsed by NASA. It is not an emergency alert system; follow official emergency-service instructions for active fire threats.
